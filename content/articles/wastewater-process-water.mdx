"Process Water" is a category of refinery wastewater loosely defined as water that has been in intimate contact with hydrocarbons. [1]

Four major sources of process water are:
1) Desalter Effluent
 - Desalters remove salts from crude oil.
 - Corrosion will occur more rapidly if salts are not washed out.

2) Sour Water
 - Water containing hydrogen sulfide and ammonia.
 - Typically routed first to sour water strippers and then to wastewater.

3) Tank Bottom Draws
 - Bottom Sediment and Water (BS&W) of crude settles during storage and must be removed periodically.

4) Spent Caustic
 - Formed from extraction of acidic components (such as hydrogen sulfide) from hydrocarbon streams. [1]
 - Routing to wastewater is one option for disposal. May be more economical to dispose off-site.

Process water contaminants include free hydrocarbons, sulfides, ammonia, and suspended solids.
