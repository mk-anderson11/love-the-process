Don’t waste energy!

This simple concept is on display time and time again in oil refineries. Wasted energy means higher energy input to the process (which increases expenses). Competitiveness and energy efficiency are closely coupled.

Let’s learn by example with regenerator flue gas of a fluid catalytic cracking (FCC) unit:

📍 Background: The FCC unit converts heavy gas oils to more valuable light products such as gasoline, olefinic LPG, and light fuel oils. About 40% of gasoline production in North America is attributed to FCC units. [1]

📍 Regenerator Flue Gas: In the FCC Regenerator, coke is burned from the catalyst. A large volume of flue gas is generated. The flue gas is hot (1300-1400 degF) and low/medium pressure (10-40 PSIG). In other words, it contains energy. [2]

📍 Energy Recovery: Since the inception of FCC technology (the first FCC was commissioned in 1942), multiple solutions have been engineered to recover the regenerator flue gas energy:
 1️⃣ Expander-Generator Combo - Uses the flue gas to drive a generator for electric power generation.
 2️⃣ Expander-Blower Combo - Uses the flue gas to spin a shaft which is coupled to an air blower, which supplies the combustion air to the FCC regenerator.
 3️⃣ Waste Heat Boiler - Generates steam using the hot flue gas

📍 For greater efficiency, \#1 or \#2 can be paired sequentially with \#3. For example, you could have an expander-generator followed by a waste heat boiler. This would result in generation of electric power and steam. (Yes, this is a lot like a gas turbine cogeneration system!!)
