Article:

Refineries cannot produce transportation fuels without hydrogen.

The table below summarizes the amount of hydrogen (in standard cubic feet, scf) per barrel of hydrocarbon (bbl) needed for common refinery operations. [1]

This helps to give a rough estimate on hydrogen consumption.

For example, a 50,000 BPD (barrel per day) gas oil hydrotreater would be expected to use about 50 MMSCFD (million scf per day) of hydrogen.

- Hydrogen consumption at refineries has increases since the 1990’s due to the increasingly stringent transport fuels requirements (such as lower sulfur).

- Reducing the sulfur content of gasoline requires a deeper level of hydrotreating, which uses more hydrogen per barrel of feed.

[1] “Handbook of Petroleum Refining Processes”, 4th Ed. Meyers, Chapter 5.2
