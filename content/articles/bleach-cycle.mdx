Bleach is a dilute solution of sodium hypochlorite (NaClO-) and water.
- It is used in refineries for wastewater treatment because it is an effective oxidizer.
- For context, Clorox disinfecting bleach is about 7% sodium hypochlorite and 93% water.

As a society, we use a lot of bleach.
- In 2015, the United States consumed about 962 million kg, or 105,600 tons. - - How is all this bleach produced?
- Where do the chlorine molecules come from?

The answer to the second question is that the chlorine mostly comes from our oceans.
- Brine, which is essentially concentrated sea water, is used to produce chlorine gas through electrolysis.
- Chlorine gas and sodium hydroxide are the two inputs to produce sodium hypochlorite.

It’s interesting that bleach decomposes over time.
- The shelf life is approximately one month.
- The concentration of the bleach will decrease as the sodium hypochlorite gradually, but steadily reacts to make salt (sodium chloride) and water.

IT'S A CYCLE!
- Chlorine gas was produced from salt water, which was used to make bleach, and the bleach then decomposes back to salt water!
